The method behind Pigboard

A feature needs more than code. It needs a clear purpose, a workable design, small changes and evidence that the result meets the need.

This is the method we use to develop Pigboard and the direction we're building into the product. The complete hosted journey is not publicly available yet.

From an idea to reviewed changes

Start by understanding the intended outcome, the people it serves and the constraints that matter. Product discovery defines success; technical discovery checks the code, integrations and assumptions.

Review the design before choosing implementation slices. Then turn the agreed scope into small, complete increments with a clear order and a concrete plan for each one.

Implement one bounded change, update its documentation and verify its behavior. Independent reviewers examine the committed code and evidence. Checks, human acceptance and release each have their own meaning.

Your team directs the product

Customers supply context, goals, constraints, budgets and approval authority. Pigboard's direction is to maintain the supported development method and coordinate work within those boundaries.

People stay involved when a product tradeoff needs judgment, scope changes or a release needs authorization. An agent completing a task does not grant it authority to expand the work or ship a release.

“Export the current filtered view as CSV first. Leave historical bulk export for later.”

That is an illustrative product decision. The intended experience keeps its reasoning with the work, so the next teammate or agent can act on it.

Progress you can inspect

A useful handoff explains what changed, how to try it, the requirements it covers, the tests and reviews completed, and the exact revision they apply to.

Queued work is not completed work. Passing tests are not a production release. A partial failure should identify what succeeded and how to continue without repeating it accidentally.

These are principles Pigboard is being built to support. Reliability and reduced coordination effort still need to be demonstrated through real delivery outcomes.
